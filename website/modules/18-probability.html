Day 18: Probability

Randomized algorithms, cryptographic keys, machine learning: CS runs on probability. It is also where intuition fails most often. Today's fix is a mechanical procedure, the tree method, that does not care what your gut says.

The Monty Hall problem

Three doors: one car, two goats. You pick a door. The host, who knows where the car is, opens another door with a goat and offers a switch. Most people say it makes no difference. The tree shows otherwise.

⚠️
Step 0: fix the assumptions. The car and your first pick are uniform, the host always opens an unpicked goat door, and he chooses at random when he has two options. A host who only offers a switch when you are right would flip the answer.
1
Find the sample space

One tree level per random step: car location, your pick, the door the host opens. The 12 leaves are the outcomes, together forming the sample space.

2
Assign probabilities

Label each edge with its probability given its parent. Multiply along a path: 1/3 · 1/3 · 1/2 = 1/18 when the host has a choice, 1/3 · 1/3 · 1 = 1/9 when he is forced.

3
Define the event

"Switching wins" is the event made of the leaves where your pick is not the car door.

4
Compute

Six leaves at 1/9 each: Pr[switch wins] = 2/3.

💡
Key Insight: Picture one litre of water poured into a river delta at the root. Each fork splits it in the labelled proportions, so a leaf's share is the product along its path. Counting leaves ("6 of 12, so 1/2") ignores how much water each one gets.
monty_exact.py 
exact = Fraction(0)
for car in doors:
    for pick in doors:
        options = [d for d in doors if d not in (car, pick)]
        for opened in options:
            if car != pick:
                exact += Fraction(1, 3) * Fraction(1, 3) * Fraction(1, len(options))
print(exact)  # 2/3
Use exact fractions so no rounding hides the answer.
Level 1 of the tree: where the car is.
Level 2: the contestant's first pick.
Level 3: the host can open any door that is neither the car nor your pick, so he has 1 or 2 options.
Visit each leaf.
Switching wins exactly when the first pick was wrong.
Add that leaf's probability, the product of its three edges.
The total is 2/3.

Uniform spaces and dice that beat each other in a circle

In a uniform space, Pr[E] = |E| / |S|, so probability is just counting. Take red dice faces {2, 6, 7}, green {1, 5, 9}, and blue {3, 4, 8}:

5/9
red beats green

One roll each, 9 equally likely pairs.

5/9
green beats blue

So red should beat blue... right?

5/9
blue beats red

"Beats" is not transitive.

37 / 2 / 42
red vs green, two rolls summed (of 81)

Wins, ties, losses. With two rolls, every matchup in the cycle reverses.
