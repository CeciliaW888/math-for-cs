Day 1: Predicates, Sets, and Proofs

Every if in your code is a tiny piece of logic. Today you get the vocabulary that lets you say "this is definitely true" and back it up.

The three ingredients of a proof

1
Propositions

A proposition is something that is true or false. A predicate such as "p is prime" only becomes one when you fill in p.

2
Axioms

An axiom is a starting assumption. Ours: standard high-school math.

3
Logical deductions

These are the steps that carry truth from the axioms to the claim. That's tomorrow's topic.

Examples are not proofs

A ∀ quantifier needs every case. A single counterexample is enough to break it.

counterexample_hunt.py 
for n in range(100):
    value = n * n + n + 41
    if not is_prime(value):
        print("Counterexample:", n, value)
        break
# Counterexample: 40 1681   (1681 = 41 * 41)
Try the claim on n = 0, 1, 2, … one at a time
Compute n² + n + 41, the value the claim says is always prime
We only care about failures, since one failure settles a "for all" claim
Report the first counterexample we find
Stop: one is enough, and 40 earlier successes don't matter
At n = 40 the "always prime" pattern breaks

What does a mathematician mean by "or"?

Chicken or pasta? (You picked exactly one on the RSVP card.)
That is XOR: exactly one, not both, not neither.
Coffee or tea? (One mug, so not both, but neither is fine.)
That is NAND: anything except both.
Cream or sugar? Have whatever you like.
Math OR is inclusive: at least one is true, and both is fine. None of the three waiter questions match it exactly.
💡
Key Insight: An implication A → B is like a product warranty. The promise is broken only if the product breaks and nobody replaces it. When A is false, the promise holds automatically.
⚠️
Common mistake: A → B is equivalent to its contrapositive (NOT B → NOT A), not to its converse (B → A). "Wednesday → pink" does not mean "pink → Wednesday."

Sets in one glance

A set cares only about membership. Use ∈ for elements, ⊆ for subsets, and set-builder notation to filter with a predicate.
