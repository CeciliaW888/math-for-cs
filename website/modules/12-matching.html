Day 12: Matching

Every March, thousands of new doctors in the US find out on the same day where they'll train. The assignment comes from a 1962 algorithm that you can prove correct today.

Three kinds of "big"

A matching pairs vertices off, with nobody in two pairs.

1
Maximal (local)

You can't add even one more edge. In the path a–b–c–d, the middle edge {b, c} alone is maximal.

2
Maximum (global)

No matching anywhere has more edges. {a, b} and {c, d} together beat {b, c}.

3
Perfect

Every vertex gets a partner. In a bipartite graph, that requires both sides to be the same size.

ℹ️
Good news: Unlike coloring, matching problems are computationally easy. There are efficient algorithms for maximum matchings and for minimum-weight perfect matchings.

Rogue pairs and stability

Applicants and evaluators each rank everyone on the other side. Two people who are not matched together form a rogue pair if each prefers the other to their assigned partner. A stable matching has none.

Watch Gale-Shapley run

Preferences: Ana ranks Mercy, City, Bay. Ben ranks Mercy, Bay, City. Cy ranks City, Mercy, Bay. Mercy ranks Cy, Ben, Ana. City ranks Ana, Cy, Ben. Bay ranks Ana, Ben, Cy.

Day 1: Ana and Ben both apply to Mercy, and Cy applies to City 🏥
Mercy: I prefer Ben, so I'll hold Ben. Sorry Ana, that's a permanent no ❌
Day 2: Ana moves down her list to City.
City: Ana beats Cy for me. I'll hold Ana. Cy, you're out ❌
Day 3: Cy moves on to Mercy.
Mercy: Cy is my top choice! Trading up. Ben, you're out ❌
Day 4: Ben applies to Bay, his second choice.
Nobody was rejected today, so the algorithm stops 🛑
Final matching: Mercy–Cy, City–Ana, Bay–Ben. It's stable ✅
gale_shapley.py 
for a in app_prefs:
    if a not in held.values():
        offers.setdefault(app_prefs[a][nxt[a]], []).append(a)
if not offers:
    return held
for e, suitors in offers.items():
    pool = suitors + ([held[e]] if e in held else [])
    held[e] = min(pool, key=eval_prefs[e].index)
    for a in pool:
        if a != held[e]: nxt[a] += 1
Each morning, look at every applicant.
Applicants already being held stay put. Only the others go out.
Each one applies to the best evaluator who hasn't rejected them yet.
No new offers means nobody was rejected yesterday, so we're done.
Return the final pairs.
Each afternoon, every evaluator looks at the people in front of them.
That includes the person they were already holding.
Keep the favourite. An evaluator never trades down.
Everyone else is rejected for good.
Each rejected applicant moves one step down their list.

Why the result is always stable

1
It stops

Every day except the last adds a rejection, and at most n² rejections are possible.

2
Everyone is paired

If A were rejected by all n evaluators, all n would end holding someone better than A. But only n − 1 other applicants exist.

3
E rejected A

E ends with someone better than A, so E won't defect.

4
A never reached E

A stopped higher up their own list, so A won't defect. No rogue pairs can exist.

💡
Key Insight: The proposing side wins. Every applicant gets their best feasible partner, and every evaluator gets their worst, all at once. Swap the roles and you usually get a different stable matching.
