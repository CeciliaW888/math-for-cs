Day 7 · Recurrences

Every recursive function hides an equation about its own running time. Today you learn to write that equation and solve it.

A recurrence is a sequence defined by induction. It tells you how to get the next term, but what we want is a closed form you can evaluate in one shot.

Towers of Hanoi in three moves

The biggest disk must move at some point, and at that moment every smaller disk has to be parked on the spare peg. That single observation is the whole algorithm.

1
Park the small stack

Move disks 1 to n−1 from A to B. This is the same puzzle, one disk smaller: T(n−1) moves.

2
Move the giant

Move disk n from A to C: exactly 1 move.

3
Restack on top

Move disks 1 to n−1 from B to C: another T(n−1) moves. Total T(n) = 2T(n−1) + 1.

The terms 1, 3, 7, 15, 31 suggest T(n) = 2ⁿ − 1. This is guess and check: the induction step is 2(2ⁿ⁻¹ − 1) + 1 = 2ⁿ − 1.

hanoi.py 
def hanoi(n, src, via, dst, moves):
    if n == 0:
        return
    hanoi(n - 1, src, dst, via, moves)
    moves.append((n, src, dst))
    hanoi(n - 1, via, src, dst, moves)
To solve the puzzle with n disks, given where they start, the spare peg, and the goal…
An empty stack needs no moves: this is the base case of the induction.
(Stop here.)
Trust the smaller puzzle to park n−1 disks on the spare peg. This trust is the inductive hypothesis.
Record the single move of the biggest disk.
Trust the smaller puzzle again to restack the n−1 disks onto the goal.
💡
Key Insight: Recursion is induction in disguise. A recursive call is not circular; it calls a smaller version you have already built and proven correct.

The master theorem: a tug of war

For divide-and-conquer recurrences T(n) = a·T(n/b) + f(n), the master theorem compares the work at the root, f(n), with the number of leaves, n^(log_b a).

1
Leaves win

f(n) is smaller than n^(log_b a): T(n) = Θ(n^(log_b a)).

2
Every level ties

f(n) = Θ(n^(log_b a)): multiply by the log n levels. Merge sort lives here: Θ(n log n).

3
Root wins

f(n) is bigger than n^(log_b a): T(n) = Θ(f(n)).

⚠️
Not a magic wand: some recurrences fall in the gaps between the three cases. Check the conditions before you pattern-match.
