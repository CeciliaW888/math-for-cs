Day 8 · Divisibility

Number theory starts here: which amounts can you build from two numbers, and how fast can you find out?

We say a divides b when b is an integer multiple of a. If d divides both a and b, it also divides every ILC s·a + t·b, because s·(jd) + t·(kd) = (sj + tk)·d.

Saving Manhattan with two jugs

A 5-gallon jug, a 3-gallon jug, a fountain, and a bomb that wants exactly 4 gallons. Watch the state machine run:

We need exactly 4 gallons. Fill the 5-gallon jug. State: (5, 0) 🪣
Pour the 5 into the 3 until the 3 is full. State: (2, 3)
Dump the 3-gallon jug, then pour our 2 gallons into it. State: (0, 2)
Fill the 5 again. State: (5, 2). Now top up the 3: it only takes 1 more gallon.
State: (4, 3). Exactly 4 gallons in the big jug ✅
Why it worked: gcd(5, 3) = 1 divides 4. With jugs of 6 and 4, every amount stays even, so 3 is impossible.
ℹ️
The invariant: every reachable amount is an ILC of the two jug sizes. It is true at (0, 0), and every fill, empty, or pour combines ILCs into another ILC.

Euclid's algorithm: cut the biggest squares

The GCD decides everything. The division theorem lets us replace gcd(a, b) with gcd(b, a rem b), and repeat.

euclid.py 
def gcd(x, y):
    while y != 0:
        x, y = y, x % y
    return x

print(gcd(606, 189))  # 3
Find the greatest common divisor of x and y.
Keep going until one number hits 0, because gcd(x, 0) = x.
Swap in the remainder: gcd(x, y) = gcd(y, x rem y) keeps the answer the same while the numbers shrink fast.
The survivor is the GCD.
 
Five remainder steps, versus fifteen with plain subtraction.
💡
Why it is fast: x rem y is always less than x/2, so the numbers at least halve every two steps. The step count grows with the number of digits, not the size of the numbers.

The Pulverizer: GCD plus its recipe

Bézout's lemma says the GCD is itself an ILC. The Pulverizer finds the coefficients while running Euclid's algorithm.

1
Start with trivial recipes

5 = 1·5 + 0·3 and 3 = 0·5 + 1·3.

2
Divide and update

5 rem 3 = 2 = 5 − 1·3, so its recipe is 1·5 − 1·3. Then 3 rem 2 = 1 = 3 − 2, recipe −1·5 + 2·3.

3
Scale to the target

Multiply by 4: 4 = −4·5 + 8·3. Fill the 3-jug eight times, pour into the 5, empty the 5 whenever it fills.
