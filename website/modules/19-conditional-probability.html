Day 19: Conditional Probability

Spam filters, medical tests and recommenders all ask one question: now that I have seen this, how should my belief change? Today you learn the maths of updating on evidence.

Rules carried over from counting

An event is a set of outcomes, so the set rules you already know still apply:

∪
Sum rule and inclusion-exclusion

Pr[A ∪ B] = Pr[A] + Pr[B] − Pr[A ∩ B]. If A and B are disjoint, the overlap term is 0.

¬
Complement rule

Pr[not A] = 1 − Pr[A].

≤
Union bound and monotonicity

Pr[A ∪ B] ≤ Pr[A] + Pr[B], and if A ⊆ B then Pr[A] ≤ Pr[B].

The spotlight: what "given B" means

Picture a dark stage with a spotlight on B. Once you know B happened, only the lit part matters, and you ask what fraction of it is also A.

💡
Conditional probability: Pr[A | B] = Pr[A ∩ B] / Pr[B]. Dividing by Pr[B] rescales the lit region so it becomes a whole sample space again. Rearranged, this gives the product rule, which is why you multiply along tree edges.

Worked example: best of three

Ash and Gary battle until someone has two wins. Battle 1 is 50-50, and after that the previous winner wins with probability 2/3. What is Pr[Ash wins the series | Ash wins battle 1]?

1
Draw the tree

The leaves are WW, WLW, WLL, LWW, LWL and LL. Each edge after the first is a conditional probability (2/3 or 1/3).

2
Multiply along paths

WW = ½·⅔ = 1/3, WLW = ½·⅓·⅓ = 1/18, WLL = ½·⅓·⅔ = 1/9 (the L-first leaves mirror these).

3
Name the events

A ∩ B = {WW, WLW} and B = {WW, WLW, WLL}.

4
Divide

Pr[A | B] = (1/3 + 1/18) / (1/2) = (7/18)/(1/2) = 7/9.

Bayes' rule: reversing the arrow

Writing the product rule both ways gives Bayes' rule: Pr[B | A] = Pr[A | B] · Pr[B] / Pr[A]. Here Pr[B] is the prior, Pr[A | B] is the likelihood, and Pr[B | A] is the posterior.

covid_test.py 
prior, p_pos_sick, p_pos_healthy = F(1, 10), F(9, 10), F(3, 10)
odds = (p_pos_sick * prior) / (p_pos_healthy * (1 - prior))
print("P(sick | +) =", odds / (1 + odds))
for _ in range(100_000):
    sick = random.random() < 0.1
    if random.random() < (0.9 if sick else 0.3):
        positives += 1; sick_positives += sick
Store the prior (10% sick) and the two likelihoods of a positive test, as exact fractions.
Odds form of Bayes: compare sick against healthy, and the hard-to-find Pr[+] cancels out.
Odds of 1:3 turn into a probability of 1/4.
Now check the answer by simulating 100,000 people.
Each person is sick with probability 0.1.
Conditioning on a positive test means keeping only the trials where the test came back positive…
…and counting how many of those people were sick. The result is about 0.249.

Reasoning out loud: the trick coin

I picked a coin at random, either the fair one or the two-headed one, and flipped heads. Which coin is it?
Prior first: each coin had a 1/2 chance before the flip.
Likelihoods: Pr[heads | fair] = 1/2 and Pr[heads | trick] = 1.
Odds of fair to trick = (1/2 · 1/2) / (1 · 1/2) = 1/2.
Odds of 1:2 mean Pr[fair | heads] = 1/3. Heads made the trick coin more believable.

Two classic traps

⚠️
Simpson's paradox: CS admits 1 of 99 women and 0 of 1 man. EE admits 1 of 1 woman and 98 of 99 men. Women do better in each department, yet overall 98 men are admitted and only 2 women. Forgotten evidence: in the jelly-tart trial, both sides argued about Pr[cake thief | tart thief] and ignored the fact that a cake was stolen. The question that matters is Pr[C | J ∩ X].
